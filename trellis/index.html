The idea in three lines

Repeatable by default

Validation, monitoring, and observability come with the platform, so every team ships AI the same safe way, and faster.

Trust by design

Explainability, audit trails, and data lineage are recorded automatically, so any AI decision can be traced and explained.

Built from reusable blocks

Teams assemble AI from shared, pre-approved building blocks instead of rebuilding guardrails each time.

Forward Deployed Engineering · the other half of TRELLIS

TRELLIS makes the governed path the fast one. Forward deployed pods put real use cases on it.

Start from the customer's problem, build with the business until it works in production, then turn what worked into a block the next team reuses. Pick a use case and follow it. Sample use cases and numbers, not Amex data.

    The loop: business problem › pod › production › reusable pattern › platform feature › the next team starts further ahead

    Scale without losing control

    Autonomy is earned, one level at a time

    Human oversight is a dial, not a fixed setting. Evidence moves each use case up; any breach steps it back down automatically.

    LevelWhat the agent doesWhat people doTo move up
    0 · ShadowRuns silently beside peopleWork as todayAgreement with people over a set volume
    1 · AssistDraftsAccept or edit; edit rate trackedEdit rate under the limit
    2 · SampledActs on high-confidence, low-risk casesReview a sample plus every low-confidence caseSample error under the limit; no drift
    3 · Guardrailed autonomyActs within allowed actions, dollar caps, required policy citationsHandle exceptions onlyContinuous monitoring; automatic step-down on breach

    Never autonomous: adverse actions such as credit or dispute denials, and decisions regulators reserve for a person. Never in the hot path: agents work beside real-time card authorization, not inside it.

    When it fails at 2 AM

    An agent invents a credit policy rule. What happens next?

    StepWhat happens
    1 · Blocked at runtimeThe answer cites no approved policy, so the guardrail stops it before a card member sees it.
    2 · Falls back to a personThe case goes to the human queue with the agent's draft and the reason it was blocked.
    3 · Circuit breakerIf uncited answers pass their limit, the use case steps down a level automatically; a per-use-case kill switch is there if needed.
    4 · On-call runbookOn-call checks what changed: model version, prompt, policy store or upstream data.
    5 · Never againThe failing case becomes a permanent evaluation test that every future release must pass.

    How the platform enforces it

    Governance runs in the request path and the delivery pipeline, so it cannot be skipped.

    Runtime path, every requestDelivery and control path, every release
    In the full version

    Policy-as-code

    One policy file per use case: risk tier, data lineage, test sets, limits, approvers, rollout, and runtime filters, enforced in CI and at the gateway.

    use_case: dispute-reply-assistant · tier: elevated · lineage: dispute-policy-kb · limits: ungrounded_pct 5, pii_exposures 0 · low_confidence: route_to_human

    In the full version

    Evaluation, rollout, agents, model risk

    • How the AI judge is checked against human labels
    • Shadow mode, canary, rollback, and kill switch
    • Least-privilege agents and audit trails
    • Model risk validation under SR 26-2 and OCC Bulletin 2026-13, and the AI inventory

    Model lifecycle in the registry

    • Onboarding: each foundation model, internal or vendor, is registered with its model card, evaluation results, and approved uses before the gateway routes traffic to it
    • Version change: a new model, prompt, or agent version re-runs the evaluation pack and the judge check before traffic moves
    • Retirement: retired versions are blocked at the gateway, and owners of dependent use cases are notified with a migration date

    Fair lending and adverse action

    • For credit use cases, the group_gap check becomes the fair lending test under ECOA and Regulation B, covering the approval-rate gap across groups and the adverse impact ratio, run before launch and monitored in production
    • A gap above the limit blocks release and goes to Compliance and Legal with the evidence
    • Adverse action: every credit denial needs specific, accurate principal reasons; the platform derives them from the explanation record and checks them before notices go out

    Vendor AI risk

    • A third-party model or vendor AI feature goes through vendor risk review and the same evaluation pack as internal AI
    • Contract terms cover data use, including no training on company data without approval
    • Re-evaluation is triggered when the vendor changes its model

    AI incident response

    • Severity levels, each with a named owner and response time
    • Escalation to Risk and Legal for higher-severity incidents
    • Customer notice and remediation where customers were affected
    • A post-incident review turns each incident into a new test case
    Proposed coverage

    AI risks we test for

    How the proposed design maps six common AI risks to the checks above.

    HallucinationThe ungrounded_pct limit at the evaluation gate, plus the grounded answers with citations block.
    ToxicityOutput filters (usage_policy) at the gateway, with severe_per_1k as the release and rollback limit.
    BiasThe group_gap_pts limit, which becomes the fair lending test for credit use cases.
    DriftDrift tracked in telemetry and monitoring; the AI judge is re-checked against human labels when a model changes.
    ExplainabilityAn audit trail and data lineage for each decision, plus adverse-action reasons for credit denials.
    Prompt injectionThe red-team set, held to the injection_success_pct limit, with gateway filters on every request.

    Building blocks teams reuse

    Each block arrives pre-approved by Risk, Legal, and Security, so using it is faster than building your own. At S&P Global, a central reusable-components team played this role.

    PII redactionMasks card numbers and personal data before any model sees them.
    Grounded answers with citationsRetrieves from approved sources; every answer shows where it came from.
    Confidence routingSends low-confidence answers to a person instead of a customer.
    Human approval stepA drop-in approval for high-impact actions, with the approver recorded.
    Agent tool allow-listScoped identity and permitted tools for each agent, enforced at runtime.
    Evaluation packHeld-out and red-team test sets, judge checks, and limits for each tier.

    Evaluation and red-teamingOwns test sets, the AI judge and its calibration, and adversarial testing of models and agents.

    Adoption, enablement, and metricsDeveloper experience, training, and the monthly dashboard; tracks friction as closely as risk.

    2025–2026 · S&P Global

    What broke, and what I changed (S&P Global)

    During my own hands-on testing of our AI-first PDLC, I found code, part legacy and part AI-generated, exposing the full server directory structure in an error response instead of a safe error. Security testing ran static scans (SAST) and only positive dynamic tests (DAST), because test data for failure cases wasn’t available. We added negative scenarios to DAST. That uncovered a second risk: log files held details that were being exposed to AI models. Both became permanent pipeline checks, with safe error handling and log scrubbing before any data reaches a model, so approvers decided from evidence instead of chasing it, and approval time on regulated checks went from 3 days to same day.
