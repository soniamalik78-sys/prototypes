One Citi: the problem and the vision

The One Citi problem. Today a customer's card, checking, lending and rewards can feel like separate companies: separate apps, separate messages, separate phone trees.
The One Citi vision. Citi knows the whole relationship and acts on it proactively: it fixes problems before the customer calls, surfaces value the customer already has, and offers credit that fits, explained in plain language.

What I believe

  • No AI chatbot stacked in front of a phone tree. AI resolves the issue or hands off to a person with full context.
  • AI never makes the credit decision. Approved credit models do; AI explains and personalizes.
  • Every proactive message must save the customer time or money, or it doesn't go out.

What it solves for the customer

  • One conversation, not three calls. Card, checking and bills handled together.
  • The real problem gets fixed. The rent is covered, not just the card ticket.
  • Nothing quietly breaks. Bills keep working on the new card.
  • No guessing. Every decision has a plain reason and a date.

What it earns for Citi

  • Keeps the card in use. Recurring bills stay on the Citi card after reissue, so spend and interchange continue.
  • Keeps the customer. A bad fraud experience is a common reason to switch; a good one builds trust in Citi as the main bank.
  • Lowers cost to serve. Fewer calls, fewer disputes for confusing merchant names, faster fraud stops.
  • Grows the relationship, later and with consent. After the case closes, card-only customers see how linking Citi checking protects them: a path to deposits and relationship tiers.

Each lever A/B tested against a control group. No offers while a customer is mid-dispute.

Architecture & responsible AI

Experience: one conversational assistant in the Citi app across card, checking and rewards; a specialist console with full context on hand-off.
One relationship view: card, checking, wealth, lending and rewards unified, migrated in A/B-tested waves, as I did at S&P Global with unified customer analytics and an A/B-tested migration; at Prudential I helped unify all customers into one dashboard.
Builder agents: requirements drafting, AI-assisted code on Citi's existing coding tools, context-aware tests, release notes.
Judge agents: score each change against rubrics for grounding, policy accuracy, security and correct hand-off; reviewers decide from that evidence.
Evaluation: golden conversations scored before and after launch; negative DAST scenarios; regression suites on every merge; latency and cost per conversation tracked.
Responsible AI: grounded, cited answers; consent and frequency caps; credit reason codes and fair-lending testing; fraud signals decide self-service eligibility; fairness compared across segments each release; designed with Risk, Controls, Compliance, Legal and Fraud from day one.
Ledger and data safety (design intent): AI quality gates block hallucinated amounts, dates or policy claims before they reach customer replies or account records; lineage to the system of record and approver logs.
Platform: Arc, Citi's agent platform, for runtime, model access, monitoring and audit; ORBIT adds quality gates and human sign-off on top. No new platform.
