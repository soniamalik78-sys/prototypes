Human–agent teams

When some team members are AI agents, three questions decide whether it works: who directs the work, who checks it, and who is accountable when it goes wrong.

Autonomy levels and accountability

Level 1 · Assist

AI suggests; a person acts. Accountability: the person.

Level 2 · Draft

Agent prepares the work; a person approves every item. Accountability: the approver.

Level 3 · Act within limits

Agent completes routine cases inside set limits; a supervisor samples work and handles exceptions. Accountability: the agent supervisor.

Level 4 · Multi-step autonomy

Agent runs a whole workflow; a named business owner owns outcomes, with monitoring, alerts and a stop switch. Accountability: the business owner.

A workflow moves up a level only when quality evidence supports it, and steps back down after an incident.

Already tested in practice

At S&P Global and DoubleVerify, AI agents did multi-step engineering work (requirements, code, tests, code review, deployment checks) under human review. An AI judge scored every change against a custom rubric so reviewers decided from evidence, and 100% of AI-generated changes had human sign-off.

One framework, localized six ways

The enterprise playbook stays the same; consultation, pacing and support change by location. All regulatory points are to be validated with Legal and local HR.

United States

Largest workforce and most use cases: start here with pilots. Align with state rules on AI in employment decisions.

Japan

Consensus-led change: involve line managers early and move at a pace that protects trust.

Brazil

Plan employee communication and data use under LGPD; involve worker representatives where required.

Mexico

Localized training and change support; data protection review for workforce AI.

India (capability center)

Scale reskilling fast; create career paths from delivery roles into agent supervision and quality roles.

Ireland (capability center)

EU AI Act treats AI used for employment and worker management as high-risk: build in its controls from the start.

Experience to draw on

Led workforce changes under the labor laws of the UK, Canada, the US and India with HR; set up two offshore capability centers; worked with teams in Mexico, Ireland, London, Paris and Tel Aviv.

Governance for AI-enabled work redesign

Every talent- and work-related AI initiative follows one pathway, scaled to its impact on people.

1. IntakeInitiative, roles affected, use case link
2. TierPeople impact: low, medium, high
3. Joint reviewPeople team, CDAIO office, Risk, Ethics, Privacy, Compliance, Legal
4. DecideHigh-impact changes go to the AI Steering Committee
5. MonitorQuality, fairness and people measures after launch

Leading people through the change

Say it early and plainly

Share what changes, what doesn't, and how people grow. Frame AI as career-building, not a threat.

Plan for the dip

Engagement usually drops first. At S&P it went 89% → 85%, then rose to 92% and 95%.

Make it safe to try

Sandboxes, champions on every team, and no penalty for honest experiments.

Handle every person with care

Consistent policies, individual conversations, and a planned path for every affected role.

The first year

Days 1–30 · Listen

Meet the Chief Talent & Capabilities Officer, the CDAIO and business leaders across the 18 business units and both capability centers. Baseline skills, sentiment and the use-case portfolio.

Days 31–90 · Design

Agree the human–agent principles and autonomy levels; stand up the governance pathway; pick 2 job families to redesign first with the agentic platform team.

Months 4–6 · Pilot

Redesign the 2 job families with the people who do the work; launch the first new roles and Academy tracks; first scorecard to the AI Steering Committee.

Months 7–12 · Scale

Publish the enterprise playbook and localization guides; extend to more job families and geographies; multi-year workforce plan with Talent and the CDAIO.
