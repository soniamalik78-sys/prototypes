Human–agent teams

When some team members are AI agents, three questions decide whether it works: who directs the work, who checks it, and who is accountable when it goes wrong.

Autonomy levels and accountability

Level 1 · Assist

AI suggests; a person acts. Accountability: the person.

Level 2 · Draft

Agent prepares the work; a person approves every item. Accountability: the approver.

Level 3 · Act within limits

Agent completes routine cases inside set limits; a supervisor samples work and handles exceptions. Accountability: the agent supervisor.

Level 4 · Multi-step autonomy

Agent runs a whole workflow; a named business owner owns outcomes, with monitoring, alerts and a stop switch. Accountability: the business owner.

A workflow moves up a level only when quality evidence supports it, and steps back down after an incident.

What the platform needs to support people safely

Agents with an owner

Every agent has an identity, a named human owner, a job description and permissions scoped to its autonomy level, registered much like a worker in the org chart.

Checkpoints as configuration

Approvals, sampling rates and escalation routes are settings in the workflow engine. Moving a workflow up a level is a reviewed change backed by quality evidence.

Evaluation built in

Rubric-based AI judges, test sets for each task and drift monitoring. Human override and error rates feed the quality scorecard.

Telemetry without surveillance

Measure teams and workflows, not individuals. Minimal data, reviewed with Privacy and Legal, with the logging the EU AI Act expects for high-risk uses in Ireland.

Skills graph

Link tasks to skills to people, so Academy tracks and redeployment target the roles that are actually changing.

Recognized standards

NIST AI RMF and ISO/IEC 42001 for AI management; OWASP guidance for LLM and agent security; least-privilege tool access for every agent.

Where I have built this
