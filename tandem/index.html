One delivery model for every channel

The Channels division is where Braze meets the end user. TANDEM is how I would lead it through the AI transition on both fronts the role describes: channel products that become more intelligent and agentic, and an engineering org that uses AI-assisted development to raise its own velocity and quality bar. It is horizontal by design, so a practice proven on one channel team is reused by every other.

Front 1 · Product

Differentiated channel products

Agentic features (conversational replies, AI decisioning, automated QA of sends) land on every channel through shared services, so each new capability is built once and positioned across the portfolio. See the platform →

Front 2 · Engineering

AI-assisted, evidence-gated delivery

Creator agents draft specs, tests and code; judge agents score them against rubrics; engineers approve. Quality score, reliability and cost per send gate every promotion. Follow one change →

Front 3 · People

Managers of managers who scale it

Clear team topology, career paths that reward building with agents, and process added or removed on evidence. Happy engineers are the foundation, not a by-product. See the model →

Why this fits Braze now

Braze is already giving marketers agents that build Canvases, test content and check every campaign against written standards before launch. TANDEM applies the same idea inside engineering: the standards a channel feature must meet are written once, checked automatically by judge agents, and approved by engineers with the evidence in front of them. It is the pattern I ran at S&P Global for 350+ engineers in a regulated business.

What stays the same for every team

  • One definition of done: rubric score, contract tests, load and cost checks.
  • One release quality score that gates each environment.
  • Human approval made fast: approvers see objective evidence, not a slide.
  • DORA metrics (MTTR, change failure rate) in every leader's goals.

What modular buys

  • A new channel is an adapter plus a test suite, not a new stack.
  • An agentic feature (say, conversational replies) is built in the core and offered on every eligible channel.
  • Reliability and cost work done once lifts every channel.

How I would approach it

  • Map today's services and ownership with senior engineers before proposing seams.
  • Pick one shared capability as the pilot, measured on latency, error rate and cost.
  • Move channel by channel behind feature flags; never a big-bang rewrite.

First 90 days

Listen first, prove it on one team, then scale on evidence.

Days 1–30

Listen and baseline

  • 1:1s with every manager and senior engineer; time with Product and Design
  • Customer calls on two channels
  • Baseline lead time, change failure rate, SLOs, cost per send
Days 31–60

Pilot one team

  • One channel team on creator and judge agents plus the quality gate
  • Agree the definition of done and the release quality score
  • Map seams for the modular platform with architects
Days 61–90

Prove and share

  • Publish the pilot's before and after
  • Second and third teams onboard from the golden path
  • Org design and hiring plan for the next year
Beyond

Scale

  • Every channel team on the same model
  • First shared capability moved into the channel core
  • Scorecard reviewed monthly with the CTO
