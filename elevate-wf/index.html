Build lab · sample assets

Every lab starts with something that works

Pick an asset and step through how it goes from one team's CX opportunity to something the next team reuses.

    What goes in the shared library

    Reusable by design, owned by name

    Agents and prompts

    Tested prompt packs and small agents, each with its evaluation set, owner and the data it may touch.

    Reference implementations

    Working code in Python or Java (RAG, agent with tools, evaluation harness) that teams copy, not reinvent.

    Playbooks and labs

    A 60-minute lab and a one-page playbook per asset: when to use it, when not to, and how to review its output.

    Choosing tools

    New tools are judged on value, risk and scale

    QuestionWhat it tells us
    Which daily task does it remove or speed up?Value, measured on one team before any rollout
    What data does it see, and where does it go?Security and data protection review, before the lab
    Pro-code or low-code?Engineers get code-first assets; business teams can get low-code flows (Power Automate and UiPath, which I have used since Prudential and Audible, or Copilot Studio), under the same guardrails
    Can we support it at scale?Fewer tools done well: at S&P Global, 8–10 tools were consolidated into shared platforms
    AI learning & development strategy

    Learning by role, on real work, measured by what changes

    A skills assessment first, so each person starts where they are. Then short, hands-on formats, close to the tools.

    Formats

    Labs and workshops

    60–90 minutes, on the team's own code and data patterns, ending with something that runs.

    Peer pairing and champions

    Each team gets a champion; people learn from a colleague one step ahead, not only from a course.

    Hackathons and demo days

    Themed hackathons (automation, security, customer centricity) and demo days; the best assets go into the shared library with their owner.

    Lived: at S&P Global, a skills assessment and peer pairing across a 350+ global organization moved engagement from 85% to 95% in 3 quarters, plus two hackathons (automation; customer centricity). At Audible, two hackathons (automation and bug bashing; security vulnerabilities) and lead champion for the CTO innovation team. At Prudential, a structured 6-month automation curriculum with prizes and awards reskilled 50+ engineers, and hands-on workshops moved business units to a new operating model.

    Adoption lifecycle

    From exploration to the way the team works

    1 · Explore

    Listen to the team; pick one opportunity with a baseline. No tool before the problem.

    2 · Pilot

    Build with the team on a reference implementation; agree what "better" means.

    3 · Embed

    Into the workflow: pipeline, IDE, runbook. Guardrails and human review included.

    4 · Sustain

    Champion named; usage and quality watched; the asset goes back to the library.
